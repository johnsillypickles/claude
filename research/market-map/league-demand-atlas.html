How to read it

  • Transplants = residents aged 25 to 44 who lived in another state or abroad a year earlier, as a share of everyone that age (Census, 2020 to 2024), with the change since 2015 to 2019. New arrivals looking for friends are a core audience for social leagues. Metros around large military bases are marked, since base rotations inflate this number.
  • Demand index (0 to 100) ranks a metro against other metros with 3+ leagues on four things: sell-out rate, waitlist signups per league, registrations per league, and new players per 100k residents aged 25 to 44.
  • Under-supplied: 60%+ of leagues sold out and index 60+. Crowded: 8+ leagues and under 30% sold out. Early: fewer than 3 leagues, too few to judge.
  • Sold out means registration filled before it closed. Waitlist counts people who asked to be notified after a league filled.
  • Cost per new player = Meta ad spend in the metro's media market ÷ first-time registrations there, Jan 1 to Oct 7, 2026.

Sources

  • Silly Pickles registrations, inventory and waitlists (Shopify, Stoq), Winter to Fall 2026.
  • Meta ad spend by Comscore market, Jan 1 to Oct 7, 2026.
  • US Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2024 5-year estimates, by metro area.
  • US Census Bureau ACS table B07001 (moves by age), 2015 to 2019 and 2020 to 2024 5-year estimates. Growth is computed on the same counties in both periods.
  • News cross-check: Redfin (Sep 2026), U-Haul 2026 midyear, This Old House, Brookings, SmartAsset 2025, UVA Cooper Center.
  • Google Trends, last 12 months, "pickleball", "pickleball near me" and "pickleball league" by media market. Retirement-heavy markets search for pickleball a lot, so search interest alone overstates league demand there.
