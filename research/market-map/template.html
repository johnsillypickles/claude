US metros (200k+ people) with no league within 30 miles

Census 2024 (ACS 5-year) and Google Trends (last 12 months), plus our own request forms. Each part is a 0 to 100 rank across this list. Transplants = share of residents aged 25 to 44 who moved in from another state or abroad in the past year, its change since 2015 to 2019, and growth in the 25 to 44 population. Military-base metros are flagged and get a neutral transplant rate. Pickleball searches run highest in Florida retirement markets, so search interest gets the least weight.

How the numbers are built

  • Sell-out = a league's Shopify inventory reached 0 through sales on a day with no manual removal. A drop to 0 with stock removed by hand (the 3-day pre-start close, or a cancellation) does not count. Spots added = stock added after launch. Seasons: Winter to Fall 2026, leagues with 10+ spots sold.
  • Density = Fall 2026 leagues per 100k residents aged 25 to 44 in the metro (Census 2024). Lower means more room.
  • Transplants = Census ACS table B07001: residents aged 25 to 44 who lived in another state or abroad one year earlier, as a share of all residents 25 to 44 (2020 to 2024), with the change vs 2015 to 2019. Metros where 3%+ of adults are in the armed forces are flagged "military" and scored neutral on this, since base rotations aren't social transplants.
  • Search interest = average percentile of Google Trends scores for "pickleball", "pickleball near me" and "pickleball league" across 210 DMAs, last 12 months. Metros are matched to the nearest DMA city, so a few border metros may be off.
  • Market cost per new signup = all Meta spend in a Comscore market ÷ first-time Shopify orders for that market's leagues (Jan 1 to Oct 5), from any channel. It's the floor: it credits Meta with organic signups too.
  • Estimated Meta CAC = spend ÷ (new signups × that market's Meta-tagged UTM share × ). The multiplier is Meta-reported purchases ÷ Meta-tagged orders account-wide, because UTMs miss most view-through and cross-device purchases. It assumes UTM capture is similar in every market.
  • Market tiers: estimated CAC vs the median market (). Efficient ≤ 0.8x, typical up to 1.25x, expensive above. Markets under $5,000 of spend are marked low data.
  • City ad set CAC (second tab) = spend ÷ Meta-reported purchases for ad sets named for a city, grouped to metro. A quarter counts if the metro had at least $1,500 local spend.
  • Index = metro CAC ÷ median local CAC that quarter (Q1 , Q2 , Q3 ). Under 1.00 is cheaper than the typical metro.
  • Consistently efficient: 2+ tested quarters, average index ≤ 0.85, and no quarter above 1.05.
  • New signups = first-time Shopify orders on that metro's league products, all channels. Local spend per new signup = local ad spend ÷ those signups, so it includes organic and national halo.
  • Demand signals (Aug 1 to Oct 7): New League Location Request form, League Host Applicants form, and "Notify me" on upcoming leagues, counted within 35 mi. Weighted 3 / 2 / 1.

Gaps and what fixes them

  • Meta's market export has no purchases, so market CAC leans on Shopify signups and UTMs. A geo holdout (pausing Meta in one market for a few weeks) would measure true incrementality.
  • No monthly split yet. Re-export with Breakdown → By Time → Month to check whether a market's CAC holds quarter to quarter.
  • of spend is "Unknown" market (likely Canada and Australia), left out of the market view.
  • Meta spends almost nothing outside league markets ( in total), so ad CTR can't be used as an expansion signal.
  • City-named ad sets mostly go to under-filled leagues, so the second tab's CAC skews high.
  • Sell-outs are measured per league, not per night. A Monday/Wednesday league counts as sold out only when every night is full.
  • Waitlist = Stoq "signed up for alert" events in Klaviyo (17,731 since Nov 2025), matched to each league's timeline. Only signups after the league sold out count; 20% belong to products with no inventory record in this window and are left out.
  • Growth is rebuilt from counties (the same counties in 2015 to 2019 and 2020 to 2024), so 2023 boundary changes no longer distort it. Connecticut metros have no growth figure because Connecticut replaced its counties in 2022.
  • News cross-check (sources linked in tooltips): Redfin (Sep 2026), U-Haul 2026 midyear, This Old House (ACS 2013 to 2023), Brookings, SmartAsset 2025 and the UVA Cooper Center broadly agree with the Census transplant data for big metros. News covers few smaller metros, so Census is the score input.
  • Meta-tagged orders = league orders whose Shopify UTM source is facebook, meta_ads, ig or similar, ÷ all orders for that metro's leagues (Q1 to Q3, all orders, not just new). UTMs catch about a third of the purchases Meta reports, so read it as a relative measure across metros, not a true paid share.
  • Le Pixel new-customer counts start in Q3 only, so the quarterly view uses Meta-reported purchases.
