How the numbers are built

  • Local Meta CAC = spend ÷ Meta-reported purchases for ad sets named for a city, grouped to metro. A quarter counts if the metro had at least $1,500 local spend.
  • Index = metro CAC ÷ median local CAC that quarter (Q1 , Q2 , Q3 ). Under 1.00 is cheaper than the typical metro.
  • Consistently efficient: 2+ tested quarters, average index ≤ 0.85, and no quarter above 1.05.
  • New signups = first-time Shopify orders on that metro's league products, all channels. Local spend per new signup = local ad spend ÷ those signups, so it includes organic and national halo.
  • Demand signals (Aug 1 to Oct 7): New League Location Request form, League Host Applicants form, and "Notify me" on upcoming leagues, counted within 35 mi. Weighted 3 / 2 / 1.

Gaps and what fixes them

  • About 58% of Meta spend (Q1 to Q3) is national, regional or creative-test and can't be assigned to a metro here. Fix: export Meta with Breakdown → Delivery → DMA, or reconnect Windsor so it can pull the DMA breakdown.
  • City-named ad sets mostly go to under-filled leagues, so local CAC skews high and the best markets (Atlanta, DC, Raleigh) barely appear in it.
  • Population and income are 2010-era (bundled ZIP database), so fast-growing Sunbelt metros are understated. Fix: allow api.census.gov in the environment's network settings.
  • Google Trends is blocked. Fix: allow trends.google.com. Then the Trends weight turns on.
  • Meta-tagged orders = league orders whose Shopify UTM source is facebook, meta_ads, ig or similar, ÷ all orders for that metro's leagues (Q1 to Q3, all orders, not just new). UTMs catch about a third of the purchases Meta reports, so read it as a relative measure across metros, not a true paid share.
  • Le Pixel new-customer counts start in Q3 only, so the quarterly view uses Meta-reported purchases.
